The stack: how people actually work

Nobody working in this field uses one AI tool. They use a small set of them, each doing the one thing it does best, handing work to the next. This article walks through the three that show up most, and the specific jobs professionals give each one.

The shape of it

Three tools, three different jobs

The reason these three appear together so often is that they barely overlap. One thinks and writes, one produces video without a shoot, and one produces everything else at volume. Work moves through them in roughly that order.

TOOL 01

ChatGPT

Think and draft

Plans the calendar, drafts the words, reads the performance data, and pressure tests the idea before anyone spends money on it.

TOOL 02

HeyGen

Video without a shoot

Turns a script into a presenter video, translates finished video into other languages, and animates a still photo into someone speaking.

TOOL 03

Canva

Produce at volume

Designs the graphics, generates images that do not exist, resizes one design into every platform, and builds hundreds of variants from a spreadsheet.

A typical week runs through all three. Monday is planning and drafting. Wednesday is design. Thursday is video. Friday the numbers go back into the first tool to plan the following week. The tools do not talk to each other automatically, which means the person moving work between them is doing something real.

The tools handle production. The person handles anything with a fact or a face attached.

Tool 01

ChatGPT

Most people first meet this tool as a caption writer, which sells it short badly. In working teams it functions closer to an analyst and a thinking partner. It reads spreadsheets, finds patterns in performance data, argues against your ideas, and holds the brand voice so three different people can post to one account without sounding like three people.

Tool 02

HeyGen

This one solves a specific and expensive problem: video requires a person, a camera, a location, and a schedule where all three line up. HeyGen removes that requirement by generating a presenter from a script, or by taking finished video and reproducing it in another language with the speaker's mouth adjusted to match.

It is also the tool in this stack with the most serious ethical weight, because it produces synthetic versions of real human faces and voices. Every use below assumes consent and disclosure.

Tool 03

Canva

Canva absorbed AI into a tool people were already using, which is why adoption was so fast. Its AI features sit under an umbrella name and cover image generation, writing, layout, background removal, resizing, and building designs in bulk from a spreadsheet.

The useful thing to understand is that most working teams ignore the majority of those features and rely heavily on about five. The ones below are the ones that come up over and over.

Practical notes

Four things worth knowing before you start

Free tiers are real but small

All three have free levels that are genuinely enough to learn on. They cap the expensive features, especially video generation and translation minutes, which is where costs concentrate.

Video pricing works on credits

Video tools often charge a flat subscription and then meter the advanced features separately. Reading how the credits work before committing avoids running dry halfway through a month.

Setup beats settings

An hour spent building a brand kit and training a brand voice improves every output afterward, far more than any individual prompt trick will.

Vendor numbers are marketing

Every one of these companies publishes research showing their tool saves enormous time. Treat those figures as advertising, not evidence, and measure your own results.

Features and pricing on all three platforms change frequently, sometimes monthly. Anything specific in this article is worth confirming on the vendor's own site before you build a budget around it.

The honest version

What the tools do not do

Every one of these platforms markets itself as replacing hours of work. The accurate version is that they get you most of the way to a first draft, quickly, and the remaining distance is where the job now lives.

01
Finish anythingIndependent testing of these tools consistently lands on the same conclusion: they produce a strong starting point and not a finished product. The last stretch is manual and it is where quality is decided.
02
Know your audienceNone of them have any idea who follows you, what happened at your organization last month, or which words your community actually uses. You supply all of that.
03
Tell you when they are guessingThere is no signal in the output that distinguishes a sourced fact from an invented one. The tone is identical, which is exactly why the checking has to be a habit rather than a reaction.
04
Get permissionA tool will happily generate a video of someone who never agreed to it. Consent is a human process and no feature replaces it.
05
Keep you consistentEvery generation is independent, so nothing stays on brand by accident. Consistency is now something you actively maintain rather than something you get for free.
