Four paths into the same industry

Every job in social media falls into one of four lanes. Once you can see them, the whole field stops looking like one impossible career and starts looking like four different doors, each with its own skills, its own pay, and its own reason to pick it.

The map

The four lanes

These are not personality types and they are not permanent. They are just the four kinds of work that have to happen for any brand to show up well online. Somebody makes it, somebody decides it, somebody measures it, and somebody pays for it.

LANE 01

Creative

You make the thing. Video, photos, graphics, and the words on screen.

$50k to $100k+
LANE 02

Strategic

You decide what gets made, when it goes out, and what the brand sounds like.

$60k to $100k+
LANE 03

Analytical

You figure out whether any of it worked, and what to do next because of it.

$65k to $100k+
LANE 04

Business

You handle the money, the deals, and the people outside your own team.

$70k to $110k+

Pay ranges are rough U.S. figures and move a lot with city, company size, and how long you have been doing the work. Treat them as a general shape, not a promise.

Pick the lane that fits how you already think. The skills come after.

Creative gets you in fastest

You can show a portfolio without anyone giving you permission first. Nobody can stop you from editing videos this weekend.

Business pays the most early

Money and relationships carry risk, and roles that carry risk pay for it. The tradeoff is that trust takes time to build.

Analytical is the least crowded

Far more people want to make videos than want to read spreadsheets. That gap is your advantage if numbers do not scare you.

Strategic is where the others meet

Most people arrive here after doing one of the other three. It is a common second job, not always a first one.

One important thing

You are not locked in

Almost nobody stays in one lane for a whole career. An editor who starts asking why certain videos work becomes a strategist. An analyst who starts pitching the findings becomes a partnerships lead. A creator who has run their own account has already done all four lanes at once, badly, which is exactly how most people learn.

Pick a starting lane, not a permanent one. The four articles that follow go deep on one lane each: the real job titles inside it, what a normal day looks like, the skills that get you hired, and where AI has already changed the work.
