Influence with impact

Everything up to this point has been about how other people do this work. The capstone is where you do it. You are going to build a real awareness campaign for a cause you actually care about, using the same tools professionals use, and you are going to finish with something you can show someone.

The assignment

What you are building

The brief

Build a social media campaign that raises awareness for an issue you care about.

Three suggested causes to choose from: mental health, climate change, or bullying. You will write the script with an AI writing tool, then turn it into a finished video with an AI video tool. The whole thing runs about twenty seconds on screen.

1 cause 1 script 1 finished video

Twenty seconds sounds small. It is not. Twenty seconds is roughly fifty words, which means every single one has to earn its place. Most people find the short version harder than a long one, and that difficulty is the actual lesson.

The path

Three steps

Here is the shape of it. The next lesson walks you through each step slowly with examples. This is just so you know where you are headed.

1

Choose a cause you care about

Pick something you would still want to talk about on a day when you are tired. Caring is not a bonus here, it is the fuel, and it shows up in the writing whether you intend it to or not.

You are not locked into the three suggestions. If there is an issue closer to your own life, use that one instead.

2

Write the script with ChatGPT

The starting prompt is simple, and it will give you something generic, which is exactly what you should expect the first time.

Starting promptCreate a 20 second script that raises awareness about ______.

The real work is what comes after. You add who the video is for, what you want them to do, and what your script should absolutely not sound like. That is where a generic draft turns into yours, and the next lesson covers it step by step.

3

Build the video with HeyGen

Your script becomes a finished video with a presenter delivering it. You choose the character, the voice, and the pacing.

Starting promptCreate a mascot that says ______.

A mascot is a useful choice here, since it avoids using anyone's real face and it gives your campaign something people can recognize from one post to the next.

Activity · Tap each cause

Narrow before you write

The single biggest mistake in this project is picking a topic and stopping there. A topic is not a campaign. Tap each cause below to see the difference between a version nobody acts on and a version somebody might.

Notice what the strong versions share. Each one names a specific person in the audience and gives them one specific thing to do. Neither of those things requires talent. They require deciding, before you open a single tool.

The bar

What separates a campaign from a post

These four things are what your finished project will be judged on, and they are worth knowing now rather than after you have already made something.

01

One audience, named

Not everyone. A campaign aimed at everyone reaches no one, because nobody hears their own name in it.

02

One action, specific

Tell one person. Read one page. Say one sentence. If a viewer cannot say what you wanted them to do, the campaign did not work.

03

One idea, repeated

Twenty seconds fits one idea said well. Five ideas said once is the most common way a short script falls apart.

04

Something honest

Every claim in it has to be true and sourced. An awareness campaign built on an invented statistic does damage to the cause you picked.

Before you start

Handle these topics with care

All three suggested causes are subjects that real people in your audience are living through right now. That raises the bar on how you talk about them, and it is not a reason to avoid the topic. It is a reason to do it well.

Four things to hold to

  • 01
    No invented numbers. AI tools produce statistics that sound real and came from nowhere. On these topics especially, every figure needs a real source or it comes out of the script.
  • 02
    No scare tactics. Fear gets attention and rarely changes behavior. It also lands hardest on the people already struggling, which is the opposite of what an awareness campaign is for.
  • 03
    Point to real help. If your campaign raises a hard subject, it should end by pointing somewhere useful. Find a current, real resource and verify it is still active before you include it.
  • 04
    Write it for one person. Imagine one specific viewer who is going through this. Would they feel seen by your script, or talked about? That question will fix most problems before they happen.
