Three ways AI changed the work

AI did not arrive in social media all at once or in one shape. It showed up in three separate places, and each one changed a different part of the job. Once you can name the three, the whole landscape of tools stops looking like chaos.

The map

Video, visuals, and words

Almost every AI tool a social media person touches falls into one of three groups. They are easy to tell apart once you know the question to ask: is this about moving pictures, still pictures, or words?

CAPABILITY 01

Smarter video creation

Tools that work on footage. They cut it, caption it, clean it up, and pull short clips out of long recordings.

  • 01
    Captions and cleanup. Automatic subtitles, background removal, and tracking that follows a subject around the frame.
  • 02
    Long to short. Scanning a long recording and pulling out the moments most likely to hold attention.
  • 03
    Speech to text. Turning what someone said into a transcript, then trimming out the filler words.
CAPABILITY 02

Eye catching visuals

Tools that make still images. Posts, thumbnails, graphics, and original art that did not exist before you asked for it.

  • 01
    Generated graphics. Post images, thumbnails, and design assets built from a written description.
  • 02
    Original art. Illustration and imagery made from scratch, useful when no photo of the thing exists.
CAPABILITY 03

Captions and content

Tools that write. Captions, blog posts, ad copy, hooks, and long threads broken out of longer pieces.

  • 01
    Short form copy. Captions, headlines, and ad text, usually many versions at once.
  • 02
    Long form and threads. Blog posts, and taking one long idea and breaking it into a sequence of short posts.

Moving pictures, still pictures, words. Every tool you will meet sits in one of those three.

The thing they share

What is true of all three

The tools in these groups look very different from each other. Underneath, they behave the same way in three important respects.

They make drafts, not finished work

Everything that comes out is a starting point. Treating output as final is the most common mistake people make with all three.

They sound the same whether right or wrong

There is no tone shift when a tool is guessing. Confidence is not a signal of accuracy, and you cannot tell by reading.

They save time on volume, not judgment

Producing forty options got much faster. Choosing the right one takes exactly as long as it always did.

They are only as good as what you ask

A vague request gives you generic output in all three groups. Being specific is the single biggest skill upgrade available.
