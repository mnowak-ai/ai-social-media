What it actually is

A presenter you do not have to book

Strip away the marketing and HeyGen does one central thing: it turns written words into a video of somebody saying them. You provide a script, choose a presenter and a voice, and the platform produces a finished video with the mouth movements matched to the audio.

The workflow is the same every time, whether you are making one video or three hundred.

STEP 01

Script

Paste your words, or upload a document the platform reads from.

STEP 02

Presenter

Choose a stock avatar, a custom one, or a mascot you designed.

STEP 03

Voice

Pick the voice and language, and adjust the pacing.

STEP 04

Export

Generate, then download in the shape each platform wants.

A video that would have taken a shoot day now takes a few minutes of waiting. That is the pitch, and it is broadly accurate. What the pitch leaves out is that the script, the angle, and the hook are still entirely yours, and those were always the hard parts.

It automates the filming. It does not automate having something worth filming.

Non negotiable

The consent rules

This is the only tool in this course that manufactures a version of a real human being. That deserves its own set of rules, and they are not complicated.

Before any video of a real person

Four things that must be true

  • 01
    Documented permission. The person agreed in writing that their likeness and voice can be used this way. A verbal yes in a hallway is not a record.
  • 02
    Script approval every time. Permission to build an avatar is not permission to make it say anything. Each script gets signed off separately.
  • 03
    A clear label. Viewers should know they are watching a generated video. The more realistic the result, the more the label matters.
  • 04
    A way to revoke it. The person can withdraw permission and have the avatar retired. Consent that cannot be taken back was never really consent.

These rules protect the person in the video, and they protect you. Every one of these steps takes minutes. Being the subject of a story about a video someone never agreed to takes years to recover from.

The honest version

What it is not good at

Independent reviews of this category converge on the same finding: these platforms are production efficiency tools rather than creative ones. They automate the filming of a simple performance, and the script, the angle, and the hook stay entirely with you.

01
Layered layoutsA presenter next to a screen recording or a product shot is not what these tools do. Generate the presenter, then assemble the layers in a normal video editor.
02
Real spontaneityGenerated delivery is competent and even. It does not laugh unexpectedly or lose its place, and those are the moments audiences tend to trust most.
03
Anything unscriptedIf the value of a video is that something real happened, no tool can produce it. Somebody had to be there.
04
Rapid creative testingSpinning up many different hooks fast to see which lands is not what this workflow is optimized for, even though the marketing suggests otherwise.
05
Fixing a weak ideaA polished presenter delivering a boring script produces a polished, boring video. The tool raises the floor on production and does nothing for the ceiling on ideas.
Practical notes

Four things worth knowing

The free tier is for testing

Free plans typically cap video length, add a watermark, and limit how many videos per month. Enough to learn on, not enough to run an account.

Advanced features run on credits

Paid plans often charge a flat rate and then meter the expensive features separately. Read how the credits work before budgeting around a monthly price.

Script quality decides everything

The gap between a good generated video and a bad one is almost entirely the writing. Time spent on the script beats time spent on settings.

Vendor case studies are marketing

Every platform in this space publishes dramatic time savings figures. Treat them as advertising and measure your own results.

Features, plan names, and pricing on this platform change frequently, sometimes monthly. Confirm anything specific on the vendor's own site before building a budget or a lesson plan around it.
