What this lane is

You are the one who checks

Every organization posting online eventually asks the same question: did that do anything? Someone has to answer it honestly, including on the weeks when the honest answer is no. That someone works in this lane.

This is the least crowded of the four. Far more people want to be on camera than want to open a spreadsheet, which means the competition for these roles is thinner and the pay tends to start higher.

What gets you hired

Five things that matter here

You do not need advanced math. You need care, and you need to be hard to fool.

01
Spreadsheet fluencyNot statistics. Pivot tables, clean formulas, and the ability to reshape messy data without breaking it. This alone clears most of the bar.
02
Asking what the number does not sayEvery metric hides something. The analysts people trust are the ones who name the gap before someone else finds it.
03
Explaining it in one sentenceA finding nobody understands changes nothing. Being able to say it plainly to a non technical boss is most of the value you add.
04
Comfort saying I do not know yetData work attracts pressure to produce a confident answer fast. Holding the line on what you can actually prove is what makes you credible.
05
Basic chart honestyKnowing how a chart can mislead, and refusing to build one that does, even when a flattering version would be easier.
