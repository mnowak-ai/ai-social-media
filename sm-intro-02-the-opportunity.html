Everyone you do not see

There are more than 50 million people around the world who call themselves creators. That number gets thrown around a lot. What almost nobody explains is that the number leaves out most of the jobs.

5 min read 3 activities Knowledge check
Start with the hard part

The odds of being the face are not great

Let's be straight with each other before we talk about opportunity. Out of those 50 million people who call themselves creators, only about 2 million earn a full time living from it. That is roughly four out of every hundred.

About 2 million full time About 46.7 million part time or hobby
Sources: SignalFire Creator Economy Market Map. Goldman Sachs Research found roughly 4 percent of creators earn more than $100,000 a year.

If your only plan is to become famous on camera, that is a rough bet. Most people who try it do not make rent from it. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

But that number only counts the people in front of the camera. It counts none of the people who made the video happen, and none of the people who do this work for regular organizations that are not creators at all. That is where almost all of the actual jobs live.

One creator you can name. Nine jobs you cannot.

Five ways in

There is more than one door

People assume there is one path and it starts with going viral. There are at least five, and four of them do not require you to be on camera at all.

DOOR 1

Be the creator

Build your own audience. Highest ceiling, longest odds, and the one everyone already knows about.

DOOR 2

Join the crew

Edit, shoot, write, or design for creators who are already growing. Steady work, and creators are always hiring.

DOOR 3

Work brand side

Run the accounts for a hospital, a college, a team, or a company. Regular paycheck, benefits, and you go home at night.

DOOR 4

Work agency side

Join a shop that handles social for many clients at once. You learn fast because you touch a dozen brands a year.

DOOR 5

Build the tools

The software that schedules, measures, and now generates the content is its own industry, and it needs people who understand the work.
