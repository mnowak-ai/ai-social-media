What the tools do

Two abilities, very different uses

Image tools split cleanly into two jobs. One is production work at speed. The other is making something that did not exist and could not be photographed.

CAPABILITY 01

Generated posts, thumbnails, and graphics

Production work. Turning a written description into the kind of image a designer would otherwise build by hand.

  • 01
    Post images. Backgrounds, quote cards, announcement graphics, and the visual furniture every account needs constantly.
  • 02
    Thumbnails. A still image built to make someone click, especially useful when the footage itself contains no good frame.
  • 03
    Design assets. Icons, patterns, and repeatable pieces that can be reused across a series.
CAPABILITY 02

Original AI generated art

Creative work. Making imagery of things that do not exist, cannot be photographed, or would cost too much to shoot.

  • 01
    Concepts and ideas. Illustrating something abstract, like a process or a feeling, where no photo would work.
  • 02
    Impossible shots. Scenes that would require a location, a crew, and a budget you do not have.
  • 03
    Style exploration. Testing five visual directions in an hour instead of committing to one and finding out later it was wrong.

The tool does not know what you want. It knows what you said.

The limits

Where image tools still fall apart

These are the failures that get spotted by audiences, which makes them the ones worth memorizing.

01
Text inside imagesWords rendered inside a generated image are still frequently misspelled or nonsensical. Add real text yourself afterward.
02
Hands and countingFingers, limbs, and any repeated object are unreliable. Count them before you post, every time.
03
Brand consistencyTwo images from the same request will not match. Keeping a whole feed coherent is now an active job rather than a side effect.
04
Real peopleGenerating a recognizable person raises legal and ethical problems that a disclosure label does not solve.
05
The sameness problemThese tools have a default look, and audiences have learned to recognize it. Specificity is how you avoid producing what everyone else produces.

Take this with you

Anyone can generate an image. Almost nobody bothers to describe one well.

The gap between a generic result and a good one is usually about twenty extra words of description. That is the entire skill, and it is available to you immediately.
